# ADR-0036 — The extraction step arrives in three parts, and still exceeds the size the plan asks for [decision]
**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-04). **Owner:** CLI.
**Context.** Extraction came to roughly a thousand lines of code in one piece. The constitution says one small pull request, a few hundred changed lines at most, and it is one of the rules that bend only with written approval. The plan gives the reason as well as the rule: review quality falls sharply past roughly four hundred changed lines, which is what makes reviewing all of it realistic. Published practice agrees and is sharper than the figure. The largest study of code review found that reviews under two hundred lines caught several times more defects than the average, and that no review larger than two hundred and fifty lines found more than thirty seven defects per thousand lines. Google's guidance calls a hundred lines reasonable and a thousand usually too large, allows two exceptions that do not apply here, and for a change that genuinely cannot be split asks for the reviewer's consent in advance while calling that extremely rare.
**Decision.** The step arrives as three parts, stacked, merged in order: the record shapes and turning a captured page into readable text; reading a command line and checking every claim against that text; and asking a model, with the instruction, the fixtures and the run done by hand. The cut follows a seam the design already had, which is the interface the model is reached through, put there for testability long before anyone counted lines. **Neither the rule nor its reasoning is satisfied even so.** The three parts are roughly three hundred and seventy, three hundred and seventy three, and four hundred and twenty eight lines of code, and no boundary anywhere in this work gets all three under four hundred. This is recorded as the written approval the constitution asks for, and it approves an improvement rather than compliance.
**Consequences.** The largest thing anyone has to read at once falls from about a thousand lines to under seven hundred, and the part carrying every correctness check is read on its own, which is what the size rule exists to buy. The cost is two more review rounds, three rebases instead of one if the slice below moves, and five open pull requests at once for a team of three. Splitting further was considered and refused: the next cut would have to be invented rather than found, and it still would not reach four hundred, so it would arrange the work to flatter a number without making any review easier. One thing is true of the three together and of none alone: the build sequence describes this step as the one AI step proven on a single real item, and only the third part proves that, using what the first two build. Each of the three says so rather than letting a reader assume otherwise.
