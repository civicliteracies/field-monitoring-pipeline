# ADR-0031 — Timing carries its supporting quote; a stored quote holds no personal data [decision]
**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-03). **Owner:** CLI.
**Context.** The store's correctness rests on one rule: a value that states a fact from a page never travels without the evidence for it. Each such field is a value paired with the exact source quote it rests on, and a deterministic check confirms the quote is a real substring of the source, so a fabricated value is cheap to catch on every push and again on rebuild. The timing field was only half covered by that rule. A dated call's deadline was re-derived from a supporting quote which was then discarded, and an open call's basis carried no quote at all. Timing is required, so a claim as real as any other could be stored unchecked: "applications are accepted on a rolling basis" is an assertion about the page exactly like a closing date, and a wrong open status is the easier error to miss, because no reader double-takes at "rolling" the way they do at a wrong date. The deadline could only be re-grounded by running the model again rather than by the cheap check that guards every other field. Separately, a stored quote sits in the structured layer, which the data rule keeps free of personal data, and a supporting sentence can carry a contact detail.
**Decision.** Timing carries its supporting quote, whether dated or open. A dated call stores the deadline and the quote it was derived from; an open call stores its basis and the quote that supports it. The deadline remains a typed date, read back out of that quote and compared to the date the model typed, and the quote is now kept rather than thrown away. Five fields therefore carry a supporting quote: budget, summary, eligibility, area and timing. A stored quote must contain no email address and no telephone number. Where a field's only support is a contact line, the field is grounded on another sentence or recorded as not stated, and the quote is refused rather than edited, because an edited quote is no longer a substring of the source. That rule is a deliberately blunt proxy for the principle behind it: it also refuses an organisation's public switchboard number, which is not personal data. The over-coverage is accepted, because the rule is cheap and checkable and a supporting quote is almost never best drawn from a contact line. Contact details stay in the raw archive, verbatim and available.
**Consequences.** Every field asserting a fact from a page's prose now carries its evidence, and timing is verifiable by the cheap deterministic check rather than only by running the model again. The record shape gains a quote on both timing shapes. The golden fixtures pin timing's quote and require every stored quote to be clean, and both fixtures already are. The cost is one stored string per record and a small check. Keeping the quote is the reversible choice, because a discarded quote cannot be recovered for records that already exist without re-running the extraction. This supersedes the earlier position that only the date is stored. It is consistent with, and does not change, ADR-0001: a deadline validates as a valid date rather than a future one, and whether a call is still open is worked out when it is displayed.
